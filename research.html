Working Papers

Printing Morality: Uncle Tom's Cabin and the Antebellum Political Realignment

This paper examines how a widely circulated cultural narrative contributed to electoral realignment. I combine historical newspaper advertisements with transportation networks to construct county-level exposure to Uncle Tom's Cabin. Difference-in-differences estimates for northern counties without recorded advertisements show larger declines in Democratic support and increases in Republican support in more exposed counties, particularly in 1856. A one-standard-deviation difference in log exposure corresponds to a 2.7-percentage-point larger decline in Democratic presidential vote share, with similar patterns in congressional elections. Household naming choices, newspaper discussions, and contemporary political references document the novel's broader cultural reception and political use. The findings suggest that cultural diffusion can help make moral arguments consequential for electoral outcomes.

Delegating Enforcement: Evidence from U.S. Immigration Policing

How does delegating federal authority to local police expand enforcement? Using the Trump administration's 2025 expansion of the 287(g) Task Force Model and newly released ICE arrest records, I employ a stacked difference-in-differences design to estimate the additional enforcement generated by local deputization. Adoption increases total ICE arrests by approximately 20 percent, and the increase extends to people with neither recorded convictions nor pending charges. Further evidence indicates larger effects in areas with greater preexisting federal detention activity. The findings identify the allocation of legal authority as an important margin of state capacity and suggest complementarities between local implementation and federal infrastructure. Tariff Interests and Political Realignment after the Panic of 1857

This paper examines how a financial crisis activated preexisting economic interests. Combining manufacturing structure with tariff rates, I show that counties with stronger pre-Panic tariff interests experienced larger declines in Democratic support and greater movement toward the Republican coalition after 1857. Congressional tariff votes also indicate increased support for protectionist legislation. The findings connect economic distress, demands for protection, and partisan realignment.

Presented: Washington Area International Trade Symposium 2026 (Poster).

Work in Progress

Redirecting Southwest China's Seaward Trade: Evidence from the Pinglu Canal

This project studies how the newly opened Pinglu Canal changes market access and connects inland economies to maritime trade. I am assembling geographic and economic data to examine how improved connectivity generates and reallocates economic activity across ports and hinterland regions, with implications for public investment and regional development.
